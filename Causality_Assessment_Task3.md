## Objective
To analyse case studies of suspected adverse drug reactions (ADRs), assess the relationship between the suspected drug and the reaction, and classify each as **Certain**, **Probable** or **Possible**.

## What is causality assessment?
Causality assessment is the evaluation of how likely it is that a particular drug caused an observed adverse event. It is a core step in pharmacovigilance: it decides how strongly a case contributes to signal detection, and it guides decisions about stopping or avoiding the drug.

## Methods used
Two standard methods are applied to every case, so each result can be cross-checked.

### 1. WHO-UMC causality system
| Category | Criteria |
|---|---|
| Certain | Plausible time relationship; cannot be explained by disease or other drugs; plausible response to withdrawal; satisfactory rechallenge if necessary |
| Probable / Likely | Reasonable time relationship; unlikely to be due to disease or other drugs; reasonable response to withdrawal; rechallenge not required |
| Possible | Reasonable time relationship; could also be explained by disease or other drugs; withdrawal information lacking or unclear |
| Unlikely | Time relationship makes a causal link improbable; disease or other drugs give plausible explanations |

### 2. Naranjo ADR probability scale
Ten questions, each scored. The total gives the category:

| Total score | Category |
|---|---|
| 9 or more | Definite |
| 5 to 8 | Probable |
| 1 to 4 | Possible |
| 0 or less | Doubtful |

The Naranjo category *Definite* corresponds to the WHO-UMC category *Certain*.

## Case 1: Skin rash after amoxicillin, confirmed by rechallenge

**Patient:** Female, 45 years
**Suspected drug:** Amoxicillin 500 mg three times daily (dental infection), started Day 1
**Other drug:** Paracetamol 500 mg when needed (tolerated many times before)
**Adverse reaction:** Generalised itchy maculopapular rash

**Timeline**
- Day 1: Amoxicillin started.
- Day 3: Itchy rash over trunk and arms, examined and documented by the doctor.
- Day 4: Amoxicillin stopped; antihistamine given. Paracetamol continued.
- Day 8: Rash fully resolved.
- 4 months later: Given amoxicillin-clavulanate elsewhere; the same rash appeared within 24 hours and resolved after stopping.

**WHO-UMC criteria**
| Criterion | Finding | Met? |
|---|---|---|
| Time relationship | Rash 3 days after starting amoxicillin | Yes |
| Other explanations | None; paracetamol continued without problems | Yes |
| Response to withdrawal | Rash resolved after stopping | Yes |
| Rechallenge | Same rash returned on re-exposure | Yes |
| Known reaction | Rash is well documented with penicillins | Yes |

**Naranjo score**
| Question | Answer | Score |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Previous conclusive reports? | Yes | +1 |
| 2. Appeared after the drug was given? | Yes | +2 |
| 3. Improved when the drug was stopped? | Yes | +1 |
| 4. Reappeared when the drug was given again? | Yes | +2 |
| 5. Alternative causes? | No | +2 |
| 6. Reappeared with placebo? | Do not know | 0 |
| 7. Drug in toxic blood concentration? | Do not know | 0 |
| 8. Dose-related? | Do not know | 0 |
| 9. Similar reaction to same drug before? | Yes | +1 |
| 10. Confirmed by objective evidence? | Yes | +1 |
| **Total** | | **10** |

**Conclusion:** WHO-UMC **Certain**; Naranjo **10 (Definite)**. Clear timing, no other cause, positive dechallenge and positive rechallenge.
**Action:** Record penicillin allergy; advise the patient to avoid all penicillins.

## Case 2: Muscle pain after starting atorvastatin

**Patient:** Male, 54 years
**Suspected drug:** Atorvastatin 40 mg once daily (high cholesterol), started Day 1
**Other drug:** Amlodipine 5 mg once daily (hypertension), for 3 years
**Adverse reaction:** Muscle pain and weakness in both thighs, with raised creatine kinase (CK)

**Timeline**
- Day 1: Atorvastatin started.
- Week 4: Thigh pain and weakness; CK about 4 times normal; thyroid function normal.
- Week 4: Atorvastatin stopped; amlodipine continued.
- Week 7: Pain resolved and CK normal. Not restarted.

**WHO-UMC criteria**
| Criterion | Finding | Met? |
|---|---|---|
| Time relationship | Symptoms 4 weeks after starting | Yes |
| Other explanations | Unlikely; no exercise or injury, thyroid normal, amlodipine long-standing | Yes |
| Response to withdrawal | Pain and CK normalised | Yes |
| Rechallenge | Not done | No |
| Known reaction | Myalgia with raised CK is a recognised statin reaction | Yes |

**Naranjo score**
| Question | Answer | Score |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Previous conclusive reports? | Yes | +1 |
| 2. Appeared after the drug was given? | Yes | +2 |
| 3. Improved when the drug was stopped? | Yes | +1 |
| 4. Reappeared when the drug was given again? | Not done | 0 |
| 5. Alternative causes? | No | +2 |
| 6. Reappeared with placebo? | Do not know | 0 |
| 7. Drug in toxic blood concentration? | Do not know | 0 |
| 8. Dose-related? | Do not know | 0 |
| 9. Similar reaction to same drug before? | No | 0 |
| 10. Confirmed by objective evidence? | Yes (raised CK) | +1 |
| **Total** | | **7** |

**Conclusion:** WHO-UMC **Probable / Likely**; Naranjo **7 (Probable)**. Good timing, no likely alternative cause and positive dechallenge, but no rechallenge, so not Certain.
**Action:** Stop atorvastatin, document the reaction, and consider another lipid-lowering approach.

## Case 3: Confusion in an elderly patient with a urinary infection

**Patient:** Female, 78 years
**Suspected drug:** Ciprofloxacin 500 mg twice daily (urinary tract infection), started Day 1
**Other drug:** Amlodipine 5 mg once daily (hypertension), for 4 years
**Adverse reaction:** Confusion and dizziness

**Timeline**
- Day 1: Admitted with fever (39 °C) and UTI; ciprofloxacin started.
- Day 2: Confused and dizzy; fever still present.
- Day 3 to 7: Ciprofloxacin continued to complete the course; fever settled by Day 4.
- Day 5: Confusion gradually improved as the infection resolved.

**WHO-UMC criteria**
| Criterion | Finding | Met? |
|---|---|---|
| Time relationship | Confusion 1 day after starting | Yes |
| Other explanations | Present; infection and fever often cause confusion in the elderly | No |
| Response to withdrawal | Not known; drug was continued | No |
| Rechallenge | Not done | No |
| Known reaction | CNS effects are known with fluoroquinolones, especially in older people | Yes |

**Naranjo score**
| Question | Answer | Score |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Previous conclusive reports? | Yes | +1 |
| 2. Appeared after the drug was given? | Yes | +2 |
| 3. Improved when the drug was stopped? | Do not know | 0 |
| 4. Reappeared when the drug was given again? | Not done | 0 |
| 5. Alternative causes? | Yes | -1 |
| 6. Reappeared with placebo? | Do not know | 0 |
| 7. Drug in toxic blood concentration? | Do not know | 0 |
| 8. Dose-related? | Do not know | 0 |
| 9. Similar reaction to same drug before? | Do not know | 0 |
| 10. Confirmed by objective evidence? | No | 0 |
| **Total** | | **2** |

**Conclusion:** WHO-UMC **Possible**; Naranjo **2 (Possible)**. Timing fits and the reaction is known, but the infection could explain it and there is no withdrawal information.
**Action:** Report as a possible ADR, monitor, and prefer another antibiotic if treatment is needed again.

## Key learning points
- A positive rechallenge is what moves a case from Probable to Certain.
- Probable needs good timing, no likely alternative cause, and improvement on stopping the drug.
- When the disease itself could explain the reaction, the case usually stays Possible.
- Using WHO-UMC and Naranjo together gives a cross-check; here both methods agreed for every case.
